the rain in zurich had the particular, greasy persistence of a november thaw, turning the cobbles along the limmat into dark mirrors that smeared the amber glow of the streetlamps. julian adjusted the collar of his trench coat as he stepped under the stone arcade of the munsterhof, shaking the dampness from his umbrella. he was ten minutes early for the dead drop, giving him time to clear his sightlines before the courier arrived.

instead, standing near the dry fountain in the center of the square, nursing a paper cup of espresso, was henrik.

julian’s stride didn’t hitch. years of field work had severed the connection between his gut and his shoulders; surprise stayed behind his ribs, a brief cold flash that never reached his jaw or his knees. he raised a hand in a lazy, half-hearted wave, letting his face settle into the tired, slightly amused mask of a man running into an old acquaintance from the vienna logistics pool.

“henrik,” julian said, closing the distance between them. “tell me they didn’t pull you off the danube desk for this.”

henrik smiled, a warm, crinkling expression that reached his eyes with easy authenticity. he extended his free hand, and julian shook it. henrik’s grip was dry, steady, and unhurried.

“just passing through,” henrik said, his voice a low, pleasant baritone. “regional audit. you know how geneva gets at the end of the quarter. everybody wants their receipts reconciled before the freeze.”

“tell me about it,” julian said, falling into step beside him as henrik began walking toward the narrow alley leading up to the lindenhof. “i’ve been chasing a phantom shipping manifest out of antwerp for three days. my knees are shot.”

“you ought to switch to those german orthopedic insoles,” henrik said casually, gesturing toward a dark doorway with his coffee cup. “miracle workers. i picked up a pair in munich last week, right after i cleared the hotel.”

julian’s eyes stayed fixed on the wet cobblestones ten feet ahead. his breathing held at a steady, deliberate rhythm.

munich.

kessler — the station chief in munich — had been found forty-eight hours ago in the trunk of a rented audi at the central train station, his throat opened with a surgical precision that left zero blood on the upholstery. the forensic file was restricted to the three-person investigation cell in langley. the cover story fed to the bavarian press was a coronary in his apartment. no one outside the langley cell knew kessler had died at the station, and no one outside the directorate knew the cleanup team had staged out of the bayernhof hotel.

henrik wasn’t cleared for that file. henrik was a mid-level logistics coordinator in vienna. there was no world in which henrik knew a cleanup had happened in munich, let alone that he had been the one to clear the hotel.

henrik was the mole. henrik had killed kessler.

keep walking. don’t change your cadence. julian’s mind locked onto the immediate problem. i know what he did. that’s the easy part. the only question that keeps me alive for the next five minutes is whether he knows i caught it.

if henrik hadn’t realized what he’d just said — if he was just an exhausted man letting two cover stories bleed together without noticing — then julian still had the board to himself. he could play the bored bureaucrat, make an excuse, get back to his hotel, and have a tactical team on henrik before midnight.

but if henrik did realize he’d just slipped, then this pleasant stroll up the deserted hill toward the lindenhof wasn’t a coincidence. it was a funnel.

julian checked his own body for tells over the two seconds since henrik had spoken. had his pupils blown? no, it was too dark under the arcade for henrik to catch that without optics. had his voice tightened? not yet.

“munich,” julian said, letting a note of mild, envious complaint enter his voice. “at least the beer is better than this swill. listen, i’d love to grab a drink, but i just realized i left my hardware token in the rental car back at the garage. if it times out, i have to call virginia for a reset, and i don’t have the patience for the helpdesk tonight.”

clean, julian thought. boring. bureaucratic. if he didn’t hear his own mistake, he waves me off and goes to bed.

“ah, brutal,” henrik said, stopping near the base of the stone steps leading up to the lindenhof park. the alley here was narrow, flanked by high, windowless medieval masonry. “which garage? the one by the bahnhof?”

“talstrasse,” julian lied smoothly, already turning his torso thirty degrees back toward the river. “five-minute walk. catch you at the debrief tomorrow?”

“actually,” henrik said, reaching into his coat pocket, “let me walk you down. i have to drop a pouch at the consulate annex on talstrasse anyway. saves me taking the tram.”

a cold needle slid into julian’s sternum.

there was no consulate annex on talstrasse. the annex was on dufourstrasse, two miles in the opposite direction across the lake.

julian didn’t correct him. correcting him would be fatal. instead, he stared at the wet cobbles and traced the logic.

why tell a lie that takes ten seconds to disprove? the only reason you invent an errand on talstrasse is to buy sixty seconds within arm’s reach of me. and the only reason you need to keep me within arm’s reach right after mentioning munich is because you know who i actually am.

henrik knew julian wasn’t really in antwerp logistics. henrik knew julian was on the langley cell. the second the word munich had left henrik’s mouth, henrik had realized he’d just confessed to a murder in front of the lead investigator.

so he knows i caught him, julian thought, his fingers tightening imperceptibly around the handle of his umbrella. if i walk toward the river with him, he puts me in the water before we hit the bridge. i have to take away the river.

“actually, you know what?” julian said, stopping and patting his breast pocket with a look of theatrical annoyance. “i’m an idiot. it’s in my inner pocket. felt the battery pack against my ribs.”

he watched henrik’s eyes. if by some miracle you really were confused about where the annex is, you stop and say great, let’s get that drink. if you’re hunting me, you have to build a new cage right now.

henrik laughed, a sharp, slightly too-bright sound that echoed off the damp stone. “well, thank god for small favors. come on, there’s a quiet wine bar just at the top of these steps. my treat.”

there it is.

henrik hadn’t blinked. he had dropped the talstrasse annex without a flicker of embarrassment — no wait, the annex is on dufourstrasse, what am i thinking? — because the annex had never been real. it had only been a leash. the moment julian cut that leash, henrik had snapped a new one onto the steps of the lindenhof.

julian’s throat went dry. now i know that he knows i’m on to him.

but does he know that i just saw through the annex lie?

julian held completely still for half a second, letting the rain drum against the canvas of his umbrella while he separated what he knew from what henrik thought he knew.

henrik had invented the talstrasse errand on the fly because he assumed julian — whose official cover was a visiting desk officer from antwerp — wouldn’t know the street address of the zurich consulate annex. to henrik, saying i have to drop a pouch on talstrasse sounded like a completely plausible, everyday excuse.

if henrik thinks i don’t know where the annex is, julian reasoned, then from henrik’s perspective, i have no idea he just lied to me. he thinks i’m sitting here believing he’s just an awkward colleague who really wanted to walk down talstrasse, and now really wants to go to a wine bar. he knows i’m terrified because of munich, and he thinks i’m desperately trying to act normal while looking for an excuse to bolt at the top of the stairs.

and if he thinks i’m still pretending, julian followed the thread, he won’t rush it. he’ll keep his hands in his pockets, keep his posture loose, and wait until we crest the steps into the dark trees of the lindenhof where nobody can see from the alley. he’ll trade speed for stealth so he doesn’t spook me before he’s in range.

unless, julian thought, a colder possibility opening beneath his feet, henrik knows i’ve been stationed in zurich before.

julian’s pulse ticked once against his collar. three years ago, before the langley cell, julian had spent four months running a temporary station out of the zurich annex on dufourstrasse. it was buried in a restricted personnel addendum, the exact same kind of file a mole in the directorate would have pulled before coming here tonight.

wait.

if henrik had pulled julian’s real file — which he had to have done, otherwise henrik wouldn’t have known julian was cleared for the munich forensics — then henrik also knew julian had worked out of dufourstrasse.

julian stared at the wet stone steps. no. nobody makes a mistake that sloppy twice in thirty seconds. or do they?

think. why would a professional operative tell a lie about talstrasse to a man he knows worked on dufourstrasse?

either henrik panicked after the munich slip and forgot julian knew the city — in which case henrik genuinely thinks his annex lie worked, and will wait until the top of the stairs to strike.

or henrik didn’t forget at all. henrik wanted julian to catch the talstrasse lie.

julian felt the hair rise on the back of his neck. why would you intentionally let your target know you’re lying to him?

because if julian thinks henrik is a clumsy liar who thinks his cover is still intact, julian will try to outsmart him by pretending to go along with the wine bar charade — waiting for a clever moment to turn the tables on the stairs. whereas if henrik believes julian is trying to pull a clever bluff on the stairs, henrik doesn’t have to worry about julian sprinting away down the alley right now. henrik gets julian to voluntarily climb into a narrow, enclosed stone stairwell with no exits, thinking he has the upper hand.

he’s baiting me into thinking i’m one step ahead of him, julian realized, the coldness in his chest suddenly total. he wants me to believe he thinks i bought the annex lie. that way i climb the steps to spring a trap on him — while he’s already got his hand on his knife in his pocket, waiting for me to commit my weight to the risers.

julian looked at henrik’s coat. henrik’s right hand was still buried deep in the wool slash pocket, just as it had been since they left the fountain. his left hand held the empty paper espresso cup.

if i’m right, julian thought, then the moment i start up those steps, he doesn’t wait for the top. he strikes on the second riser, the instant my back turns, because he knows i know everything and he knows i’m only climbing those stairs to set him up.

and if i’m wrong — if he really did just panic and forget i knew dufourstrasse — then he expects me to play along until we reach the trees.

either way, julian couldn’t give him the stairs.

julian didn’t step onto the first riser. instead, he let his umbrella tilt slightly forward, letting the rainwater run off the ribs onto the cobbles between them, and smiled — not the tired bureaucrat’s smile from the square, but a flat, quiet look that dropped the antwerp cover entirely.

“you know what’s funny about the annex, henrik?” julian said softly, his weight shifting onto the balls of his feet, his right thumb sliding down the shaft of the umbrella to the steel release catch. “the espresso machine in the basement on dufourstrasse has been broken since twenty-three. i don’t know why you didn’t just get your coffee there.”

for a fraction of a second, neither of them moved.

julian watched henrik’s face, waiting to see which version of henrik was standing in front of him.

if henrik had genuinely forgotten julian knew the city, his eyes would widen in shock — the sudden, catastrophic realization that his cover had been blown twice over and julian had been playing him since the alley.

henrik’s eyes didn’t widen.

instead, the warm, crinkling smile simply vanished, like a light switched off behind a curtain. henrik didn’t look surprised at all. he looked mildly disappointed, the way a chess player looks when an opponent declines a poisoned pawn and forces a grind into the endgame.

“i figured you’d spot that,” henrik said, his pleasant baritone dropping half an octave into something flat and entirely devoid of warmth. his right hand didn’t even bother to stay hidden anymore; the wool of his coat pocket pulled taut as his thumb clicked a safety catch beneath the fabric. “honestly, i was hoping you’d be arrogant enough to take the stairs anyway.”

“i almost did,” julian admitted, keeping his eyes locked on the angle of henrik’s right shoulder, measuring the distance to the masonry wall behind him. “until i realized nobody’s that bad at geography.”

“fair enough,” henrik said, shifting his front foot half an inch on the wet stone, his empty paper cup dropping from his left hand and bouncing softly against the cobbles. “so. no more pretending.”

“no more pretending,” julian agreed, and drove the tip of the umbrella straight toward henrik’s throat before the paper cup even hit the ground.
