# The “Henry County Test”: What Happens When an Independent Watchdog Actually Bites?

### Election by Jury Hits the Pavement

For years, skeptics have asked the same question: _“Sure, a grand jury-appointed ethics board sounds good on paper—but what happens when they actually have to fight? Will they fold when the political pressure gets real?”_

We now have the answer. And then some.

What began in Henry County, Georgia as a test of structural independence has become something more: a documented, step-by-step case study in how a political establishment dismantles an independent oversight mechanism once it starts working. The full story is now in the public record. The bill text is confirmed. The timeline is damning.

* * *

## The Setup: Who Is Being Investigated

**Carlotta Harrell** is the Chairwoman of the Henry County Board of Commissioners—the highest-ranking elected official in the county. In most jurisdictions, an ethics investigation of someone with that much power over budgets, careers, and county contracts quietly dies. The investigators either work for her or fear her.

Henry County was supposed to be different.

In 2021, **State Senator Emanuel Jones** sponsored Senate Bill 22, which created the Henry County Board of Ethics with a deliberate structural independence mechanism: five of the board’s seven members would be appointed by the **grand jury** —citizens selected by lot, owing nothing to any politician. One appointee per commission district. The grand jury selections would be published. Their terms would be staggered.

Jones called it a model for accountability. He was right. Which is why what happened next is so instructive.

* * *

## Act I: The Walkout

In late 2025, the Ethics Board convened to investigate **Scott Morris** , the county’s Transportation Director, on ethics allegations. They issued lawful subpoenas for nine county employees to testify as witnesses.

Midway through the proceedings, Ethics Administrator Gloria Banister was pulled aside. At timestamp 46:48 in the [hearing video](https://youtu.be/qo7HQKgtW8I?t=2705), she returned to the room and delivered the message on the record:

> “I’m sorry, I do redact... It was not the County Manager’s request, **it was Chair Harrell’s request**... the County Attorney advised them that the Henry County Board of Ethics does not have the authority to subpoena employees.”

The nine subpoenaed witnesses stood up and walked out.

* * *

## Act II: The Counter-Punch

In most counties, the walkout would have ended it. The board would have accepted the implicit message— _you don’t have the power here_ —and moved on.

The Henry County Ethics Board didn’t blink.

Board Chair **Felicia Harrell** (no relation to Carlotta) and her four grand-jury-appointed colleagues—Jeffrey Lee, Dr. Samuel Valme, Diane Easter, and Dr. Jazmin Willingham—voted to launch a formal investigation into Chairwoman Carlotta Harrell herself for obstruction and inappropriate conduct.

They could do this because none of them owe their seats to the Commission Chair. Grand jury appointment means no political patron to protect. Carlotta Harrell had no leverage over any of them.

Chairwoman Harrell’s public response: _“They can investigate all they want to, but I will not be answering anything that they put out.”_

* * *

## Act III: Administrative Strangulation

When legal intimidation failed, the establishment moved to physical attrition.

Because Chairwoman Carlotta Harrell controls the county checkbook, she could starve the very board investigating her. At their [January 13, 2026 meeting](https://henrycountyethics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/1.13.26-AGENDA-1.pdf), the Board documented the campaign:

  * **Budget denial.** The Ethics Administrator confirmed that posting a single job listing for a conflicts attorney cost $310 per week—money they didn’t have. They used a free trial on Indeed.

  * **Space denial.** The county barred the board from the Community Room (outfitted for broadcasting) and confined them to a cramped office that couldn’t legally hold a public hearing.

  * **Equipment denial.** The county Communications Department refused to let the board use available cameras to stream their meetings.




Board Chair Felicia Harrell stated on the record: _“So basically they want the Board of Ethics to be a figurehead and not an active engaged board because... we’re being limited in every way.”_

* * *

## Act IV: Proof of Independence

Critics argued the board was a political hit squad, weaponizing ethics complaints against Harrell’s allies.

The board answered that argument at the same January meeting. A separate complaint—Case 2026-EB-1—had been filed against **County Manager Cheri Hobson-Matthews** for alleged nepotism involving her husband and sister. If the board wanted political blood, here was an easy target.

Instead they followed the evidence. The investigation revealed her husband was hired in 2004—thirteen years before she became county manager. The board voted unanimously to dismiss the case.

When the evidence wasn’t there, they dropped it. When it was there, they held.

* * *

## Act V: The Gaslighting, and the Bill Built to Replace It

While the administrative war unfolded, Senator Jones issued a public statement in December 2025 citing **Senate Bill 359** —legislation he had sponsored—as authority for the proposition that the ethics board had no jurisdiction over county employees like Scott Morris.

There was a problem: the bill he was citing didn’t say that.

The original SB 359 had passed the Senate 49-0 on March 31, 2025 and been sitting in the House since April. It contained no explicit prohibition on employee complaints. It did not strip grand jury appointments. Jones was asserting authority the bill’s actual text did not provide—banking on the fact that almost no one would read it.

The board didn’t back down anyway.

So the establishment did something more consequential than cite a bill. They rewrote it.

On March 31, 2026—after months of the board refusing to fold—the House passed a substitute version of SB 359 by a vote of 161 to 3. The Senate agreed to the substitute on April 3, 44 to 6. The enrolled bill was sent to Governor Brian Kemp on April 10.

The substitute is the legal weapon Jones had been pretending already existed. Read next to the original, it’s a confession of what the gaslighting was for.

* * *

## Act VI: What the Substitute Actually Does

We have the enrolled bill text. Here is what it does, confirmed line by line.

**It eliminates grand jury appointments entirely.**

The old board had seven members, five appointed by the grand jury. The new board has five members total. Under lines 263–269, each seat belongs to one appointing authority:

  * The Clerk of the Superior Court of Henry County

  * The Judge of the Probate Court of Henry County

  * The Sheriff of Henry County

  * The Tax Commissioner of Henry County

  * The Solicitor-General of Henry County




Every one of these officials is an elected county constitutional officer. The grand jury disappears not just in majority but entirely—zero of five seats remain. The sortition mechanism Jones built in 2021 is gone.

**It does this silently.**

The bill completely rewrites Section 7.1 of the county charter. The phrase “grand jury” does not appear anywhere in the new text. No provision says the grand jury appointment system is being repealed. It simply vanishes, overwritten. Anyone reading the bill’s official title—”revise and restate provisions regarding the code of ethics”—has no way of knowing what was actually removed.

**It explicitly bars the original investigation.**

Lines 331–335 restrict the board’s jurisdiction to “elected and appointed county officials” and add: “the board of ethics shall not accept or hear complaints of violations of this code of ethics against **county employees**.”

Scott Morris, the Transportation Director whose alleged misconduct triggered this entire chain of events, is a county employee. Under SB 359, the investigation that started everything is not just jurisdictionally questionable—it is **explicitly prohibited by statute**. The substitute was written to make retroactively illegal the precise thing the board was doing when it refused to stand down.

**The new board takes office January 1, 2027.** The current independent board is dissolved.

* * *

## Act VII: The Smoking Gun

On April 22, 2026—while SB 359 was enrolled and awaiting Kemp’s signature—Chairwoman Carlotta Harrell fired County Manager Cheri Hobson-Matthews unilaterally, without a board vote.

Section 2 of SB 359 (lines 507–508) requires a majority vote of the board of commissioners to fire the county manager. Harrell acknowledged this to the Atlanta Journal-Constitution, noting she had the authority to act unilaterally “though under new legislation that is awaiting the governor’s signature, that decision would require a majority vote by the board.”

She fired the county manager while she still legally could—in the window between the bill’s passage and its signing. She knew the window was closing. She used it.

This is not a coincidence. It is proof that the people who designed SB 359 understood exactly what it was for, and were already acting on that understanding before it was law.

* * *

## What This Is

The sequence tells the whole story.

Senator Jones builds a grand jury appointment system in 2021. The board it creates, staffed by citizens who answer to no politician, investigates a powerful official in 2025. Jones cites legislation to stop the investigation—legislation whose actual text doesn’t support his claim. The board calls his bluff by refusing to stop. A substitute is then written that makes the bill do what Jones said it already did, strips the grand jury appointments entirely, and explicitly bars the investigation that triggered everything. It passes both chambers by overwhelming margins. The targeted official preemptively fires a county employee in the window before the bill is signed.

This is structural capture. It is documented. It is in the public record.

The model worked exactly as designed. That is precisely why the establishment destroyed it.

* * *

## Two Days Left

Governor Kemp’s deadline is May 12. If SB 359 becomes law, the grand jury-appointed board is dissolved on January 1, 2027, replaced by a board whose every member answers to an elected politician. The ethics investigation of Carlotta Harrell will almost certainly be terminated. Any future investigation of county employees—the original trigger—will be explicitly prohibited by statute.

Governor Kemp’s office: **404-656-1776**
