TITLE
the case against voting

A/B ALTERNATES
nine witnesses walked out
what if a random jury picked the mayor?

DESCRIPTION
In Henry County, Georgia, an ethics board subpoenaed nine county employees, and they walked out on orders from the commission chair. The board didn't fold. It opened an investigation into the chair herself. It could do that because most of its members weren't chosen by voters or politicians. They were chosen by a grand jury.

This video makes the case for election by jury: instead of asking millions of people to cast a vote they have almost no reason to research, draw a random, paid, representative jury, give it a week or two to hear every candidate, and let it pick the winner by secret ballot, using score voting.

We cover the two jobs any way of choosing leaders has to do (alignment and competence), why mass elections fail at both, how big a jury needs to be, why random selection resists bribery, Athens and Venice, how Georgia grand juries already appoint everything from tax appeal boards to a power utility that runs its own dam, the 2017 attempt to bring back grand-jury-appointed school boards, the Supreme Court case that shows why the randomness has to be real, and what happened when the Georgia legislature rewrote the law to get rid of Henry County's jury-picked ethics board.
