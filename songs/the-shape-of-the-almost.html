the bridge is two four-bar passes in E minor. they're identical until the last bar of each.

pass one: a minor-key cadence

the bass walks A, G, C, B: subdominant, tonic, submediant, dominant. the B7(b9) borrows from harmonic minor. its D# is the raised leading tone that pulls up to E, and its C is the flat 9, taken straight from natural minor. it's a textbook dominant, and the 5/4 bar stretches it so you wait for the resolution.

pass two: F#maj7 over an E bass

the second time, that dominant is replaced, and the new chord does three things at once.

  1. the root jumps a tritone. C to F# is the most distant root move there is, so after three bars of predictable diatonic motion the floor drops out.
  2. over the E bass, the F# triad is E lydian color. F#, A# and C# are the 9th, #11 and 13th of E, the bright, floating sound of a major chord a whole step up over the root. the chord's 7th, E# (enharmonically F), sits a minor 9th above the bass E. that's the bite: a half-step rub inside an otherwise glowing chord.
  3. it resolves into the chorus's Em7 by pure half steps. the bass E is already the tonic, so it holds, and every upper voice moves one semitone: F# to G, A# to B, C# to D, E# to E. the most distant chord in the progression lands on the tonic with the smallest possible motion.

so it does a dominant's job, leading tones pulling into the tonic, without being a dominant. the first pass resolves home the expected way. the second looks like it's about to go somewhere else entirely, flashes a bright lydian light over the tonic, then slips back into E minor by half steps.
